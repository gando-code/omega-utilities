# Bell-Inequality Entanglement Telegraph (BIET)

## Theorem
Bell's Theorem (1964) and the CHSH inequality: |S| ≤ 2 for any local realistic theory; quantum mechanics allows S ≤ 2√2 (Tsirelson bound). Loophole-free violations (Hensen et al., 2015) confirm nature is nonlocal. Device-independent QKD (Ekert, 1991) uses Bell violation itself as the security guarantee — not the math of key exchange.

## Construction (Civilian Tier)
Two parties share entangled pairs. They measure in random bases and compute the CHSH correlator S. If S > 2, the channel is certified secure by physics — no man-in-the-middle, even with unlimited computation, can reproduce the correlations. The key is extracted from the Bell-violating outcomes.

## Civilian Use Cases
- **Tamper-proof communications:** Security guaranteed by quantum nonlocality, not by math assumptions.
- **Physics-certified key distribution:** The key is valid iff the Bell inequality is violated.
- **Device authentication:** A device is authentic iff it can produce Bell-violating correlations.

## Limitations
Civilian tier requires a quantum channel. Classified and omega tiers (restricted repo) handle device-independent protocols robust to adversarial devices and inter-civilization entanglement distribution.
