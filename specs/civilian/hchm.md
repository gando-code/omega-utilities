# Holographic Causal Horizon Mapper (HCHM)

## Theorem
Causal set theory (Bombelli–Sorkin, 1987): spacetime is fundamentally a discrete partially ordered set; the causal order *is* the geometry. The holographic principle: the information available to an observer is bounded by the area of their causal horizon. Together: each observer has a well-defined causal horizon — the boundary of what they can ever know.

## Construction (Civilian Tier)
Given an observer's worldline and a causal model of the system, the mapper computes the causal horizon: the set of events that can ever influence the observer (past horizon) and the set the observer can ever influence (future horizon). Events beyond the horizon are mapped as "forever unknowable." The output is a causal horizon diagram for strategic planning.

## Civilian Use Cases
- **Strategic planning boundaries:** Know what you can ever know, and what is forever beyond reach — plan accordingly.
- **Decision theory:** Make decisions that account for the irreducible unknowns on your horizon.
- **Information diet:** Stop seeking information beyond your causal horizon; it cannot reach you.

## Limitations
Civilian tier maps horizons for classical causal models. Classified and omega tiers (restricted repo) map intelligence horizons and the observable-universe boundary for inter-civilization strategy.
